# Operator Interview Guide — Field Questions for Real Operators

**Purpose:** convert the assumptions in [`next-gen-experience-platform-vision-2026-09.md`](./next-gen-experience-platform-vision-2026-09.md) into questions that real operators can answer from memory, about things that already happened.
**Companion to:** [`validation-prompts-memory-layer-vision-2026-09.md`](./validation-prompts-memory-layer-vision-2026-09.md) — that file tests the *document*; this file tests the *market*.
**Discipline:** Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*. Talk about their life, not your idea.
**Version:** 1.1 — adds §18 (Spain and Andalucía regulatory reality) and §19 (Andalusian operating calendar) to test the risks raised in vision §17. **Read §18 before any interview conducted in Andalucía.**

---

## 1. The three rules

**Rule 1 — Never pitch.** If the operator learns what you are building, every subsequent answer is contaminated by politeness. Pitch only in the final two minutes, after the data is collected.

**Rule 2 — Past facts only.** "How many messages did the last one take?" is data. "Would you use something that reduced messages?" is noise. Every question below is written in the past tense for this reason.

**Rule 3 — Ask for the artefact.** The strongest moment in any of these interviews is *"can you show me?"* Their phone, their spreadsheet, their WhatsApp thread, their last invoice. What people show you is true; what they describe is edited.

**Practical notes:** 35–45 minutes. Record only with explicit spoken consent, and delete on request — you are asking EU operators about their business, so model the data discipline you are selling. Conduct in Spanish where the operator prefers it (see §11). Take notes on what they *do*, not what they *think*.

---

## 2. Screening — place them in a segment in four questions

Ask these first. They take ninety seconds and determine which block you run.

1. "How many tours did you personally guide last month?"
2. "Who else guides for you — employees, freelancers, or nobody?"
3. "Roughly what share of your bookings came through Viator, GetYourGuide or similar last season?"
4. "When a guest wants something that isn't on your menu — a private day in Granada, say — what happens?"

| Answers | Segment | Run block |
|---|---|---|
| Guides most tours, no team, low OTA share | **S1 — Solo bespoke operator** | §4 |
| Guides for several operators, doesn't own the bookings | **S2 — Licensed freelance guide** | §5 |
| 5–20 guides, private *and* scheduled group | **S3 — Mid-size company** | §6 |
| 70%+ from OTAs | **S4 — OTA-dependent operator** | §7 |
| High-value, multi-day, concierge-style, often FR/AR/US clients | **S5 — Luxury / DMC** | §8 |
| Routinely passes work to operators in other cities | **S6 — Network node / broker** | §9 |

Most real operators are two of these at once. Run the primary block, then cherry-pick from the secondary.

## 3. Block A — Universal opening (every segment, always)

These eight questions carry most of the value in the entire guide. If an interview gets cut short, you still have what you need.

**A1. "Walk me through the last private booking you closed. Start from the first message."**
*Then shut up.* Let them narrate. Everything you need about qualification cost, quoting, deposits and tooling is inside this one story.

**A2. "Can you open that conversation on your phone and count the messages?"**
The single most valuable number in the interview. The vision claims 10–20. Find out.
→ *Falsifies the wedge if the honest answer is routinely under 5.*

**A3. "How long between their first message and your quote? And between your quote and their yes?"**
Tests whether speed or effort is the real problem. They are different products.

**A4. "What did you have to look up or calculate before you could send that price?"**
Tests the "zero-typing quote". If they quote from memory in ten seconds, that feature is dead.

**A5. "Tell me about an enquiry last month that you spent time on and never converted. What happened?"**
Unpaid discovery work is where the pain actually lives. Ask what they *did*, not how it felt.

**A6. "Where are your last ten bookings from? Go through them one at a time."**
Forces real attribution instead of a guess. Tests the referral and repeat-rate assumptions directly.

**A7. "When was the last time a booking went wrong on the day? Tell me exactly what happened."**
Do not prompt with ID, meeting point or weather. Let them choose the failure. What they name first is the real #1 problem — the vision *assumes* it is ID and meeting point.

**A8. "What do you do in August?"**
Seasonality in one question. Listen for whether they close, discount, or have simply accepted the loss.

## 4. S1 — Solo bespoke operator (the stated beachhead)

**Tools and the dashboard thesis**
- "Show me every app you opened for work yesterday."
- "When did you last log into your booking system, and what did you go in to do?"
- "What is the most annoying thing you have to do on a computer rather than your phone?"
- "Has anyone sold you software in the last two years? What happened to it?" → *the single best predictor of whether they will adopt anything.*

**Money and channel**
- "What did you pay in OTA commission last season, roughly?"
- "Of that, how much would you say you could have won direct?"
- "Do you take a deposit? … Why not?" (If they do: "What changed that made you start?")
- "How many no-shows or late cancellations last season, and what did they cost you?"

**Reviews and referral**
- "How do you ask for reviews? Show me the message you send."
- "Of your last twenty reviews, how many came from you asking?"
- "Has a guest ever sent you another guest? How did you find out?"

**Repeat**
- "Has any guest ever come back, or booked you in another city? Tell me about them."
- "Do you have any way to contact past guests? Show me."
→ *If they have no list at all, the entire back half of the loop has no foundation and that is a finding, not a failure.*

**Legal and admin**
- "How often does someone ask you for a *factura completa*? What do you do?"
- "Who does your invoicing?"

## 5. S2 — Licensed freelance guide (**run this at least three times — it tests the load-bearing bet**)

The Guide's Gift is the assumption the whole memory thesis rests on. It cannot be validated by talking to owners. **Talk to guides.**

**The end of the tour — the critical sequence**
- "Take me through the last twenty minutes of your last tour. From the last stop to walking away."
- "What is the first thing you do after the guests leave?"
- "How many tours did you do that day? Was that the last one?" → *the 90-second ask lands on tour four, not tour one.*
- "Where is your phone during a tour? What's the battery like at the end?"

**Photos and stories — ask without revealing why**
- "Do you take photos on tour? Of what? Show me the last one."
- "Has a guest ever asked you to send them a photo? What did you do?"
- "Has a guest ever asked you to write down something you said — a name, a date, a story?"
- "Is there a story you tell at the same spot every time? … Would you mind telling it to me now?"
→ *If they light up telling it, the feature has emotional fuel. If they are bored of it, ask why.*
- "Have you ever recorded a voice note for a guest? For anyone at work?"

**Incentive and status**
- "Do guests ever mention you by name in reviews? How do you know?"
- "Does that get you more work? From whom?"
- "How many different operators do you guide for?"
- "If an operator asked you to do one extra thing after every tour, what would make you refuse?"
→ *The answer to this last question is the design brief for the Guide's Gift.*

**Reality check**
- "What's the last thing an operator asked you to do that you quietly stopped doing?"

## 6. S3 — Mid-size company (5–20 guides)

**Scheduling — their real daily pain**
- "How did you staff last Saturday? Walk me through it."
- "When did you last have a booking you couldn't staff? What did you do?"
- "How do you know which guide can work inside which monument, in which language?" → *ask to see it. Usually a spreadsheet or someone's head.*

**Systems**
- "Which system holds the truth about your availability?"
- "When did two channels last disagree? What happened?"
- "Who in the company touches the booking system? How many hours a week?"
- "What reports do you actually look at? Show me the last one you opened."

**Private vs group**
- "Does your private product cannibalise your group product, or feed it?"
- "Which is more profitable per guide-hour? Do you know, or is that a feeling?"

**Buying behaviour** *(this segment buys differently — find the process)*
- "Who signs off on new software? What did the last approval look like?"
- "What software did you buy last year and what did it replace?"
- "What are you already paying per month across all your tools?"

## 7. S4 — OTA-dependent operator

This segment is where most of the *market* is and where the pitch is most likely to fail. Interview them precisely because they are the hardest.

- "What share of last season came through OTAs? What did that cost you in euros?"
- "Have you ever tried to shift guests to direct? Tell me exactly what you did and what happened."
- "Do you know the email address of any guest who booked through Viator?"
- "What would your OTA account manager say if they knew you were talking to me?" → *tests fear of retaliation and rate-parity constraints, which the vision document does not address at all.*
- "Last time a guest found you *after* an OTA tour and booked direct — what did they say?"
- "What would have to be true for you to turn off one OTA channel?"

**The reality you must surface:** many OTA-dependent operators do not *want* the direct relationship. They want volume without marketing. If that is true at scale, the entire thesis narrows to a premium bespoke niche — which is survivable, but must be known now.

---

## 8. S5 — Luxury / DMC

- "Tell me about your last multi-day client. How did they find you?"
- "How much of that itinerary did you write by hand?"
- "Has a client ever taken your itinerary and booked it elsewhere?"
- "How much do you spend on a client before they have paid you anything?"
- "What language do you work in with Gulf clients? Who handles that?"
- "What do you give clients at the end? Anything physical?" → *directly tests appetite for a printed keepsake in the segment most able to pay for it.*
- "Does a client ever come back a second year? What made them?"
- "What do you send a past client in December?"

---

## 9. S6 — Network node / broker

Tests the Intent Passport and the attribution ledger.

- "Last time you sent a client to an operator in another city — how did you hand over the details?"
- "Can you show me that handover message?" → *this is the Intent Passport's real-world baseline.*
- "What went wrong with a partner handover? Tell me the worst one."
- "How do you get paid on that? When?"
- "Has a partner ever kept a client you sent them? How did you find out?"
- "How do you choose which partner gets a request?"
- "Would you let a partner see what your guest told you, word for word?" → *tests whether intent-sharing feels like value or like exposure. If operators guard the brief, the network moat is weaker than the document assumes.*

---

## 10. Cross-segment: the memory thesis

Operators cannot validate traveller demand — only travellers can. But they hold three usable signals:

- "Have guests ever sent you their photos after a tour?"
- "Has a guest ever sent you something months or years later?" → *ask to see it. This is the closest available proxy for whether the memory has emotional value.*
- "Do you sell gift vouchers? How many last year? Who bought them — the traveller or someone else?"
- "Has anyone ever asked you for a written record of their trip?"

**Interpret honestly.** These questions test whether operators have *noticed* the behaviour, not whether the behaviour exists. Weak answers here are weak evidence, not disproof. Proper validation needs traveller interviews — a separate study.

## 12. Never ask these

Each of these reliably produces a polite lie.

| Do not ask | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| "Would you use a tool that qualifies your enquiries?" | "How many messages did the last one take?" |
| "Would you pay for this?" | "What did you pay for X last year?" |
| "Do you think guides would record a 30-second story?" | "What's the last thing an operator asked you to do that you quietly stopped doing?" |
| "Is it annoying that OTAs keep the guest's email?" | "Do you know the email of any guest who booked through Viator?" |
| "How important are reviews to you?" | "Show me the message you send to ask for one." |
| "Do you like your booking system?" | "When did you last log in, and what for?" |
| "Would travellers value a memory of their trip?" | "Has a guest ever sent you something months later?" |
| Anything beginning "Do you think…" | Anything beginning "Tell me about the last time…" |

## 13. Listening guide

**Good data — write it down verbatim**
- A number they can produce from their phone.
- A workaround they built themselves. People only build workarounds for real pain.
- Money already spent on a partial solution.
- An emotional, specific, unprompted story.
- "Let me show you."

**Bad data — discount it**
- Compliments about you or the idea.
- "I would always / I would never" — futures and generalities.
- Feature requests. Record the *problem behind* the request, discard the request.
- Enthusiasm with no number attached.

**Red flags for the thesis**
- The last booking took three messages, not fifteen.
- They log into their booking system daily and like it.
- Guides refuse any post-tour task, at any length, for any reason.
- Nobody has ever been denied monument entry for missing ID.
- They do not want the direct relationship — they want volume without marketing.
- They guard the guest brief and will not share it with a partner.

**Buying signals that actually mean something**
- They ask when it will be ready — unprompted.
- They introduce you to another operator during the call.
- They offer their own data or their season to test it.
- They name a price before you do.

## 14. The three commitment asks

End every interview with one of these. An interview with no commitment ask produced entertainment, not evidence. Escalate in order.

1. **Time:** "Can I sit with you for one morning and watch how enquiries come in?"
2. **Reputation:** "Who else should I speak to? Would you introduce me?"
3. **Money or access:** "Would you let me handle your next five enquiries and measure it against your normal?"

A "yes, but later" is a no. Record which of the three they accepted — that column is the truest one on your scoring sheet.

## 17. Stop rule

**Run at least:** 6 × S1, 3 × S2 *(non-negotiable — this is the load-bearing bet)*, 2 × S3, 2 × S4, 1 × S5, 1 × S6. Fifteen interviews.

**Stop early if:** three consecutive interviews produce no new information and no falsified assumption. You have found the pattern; go build the test.

**Stop and rethink if:** the median answer to A2 is under five messages. That is the wedge, and without it the sales motion in §12 of the vision document does not exist.

**Do not stop because:** everyone was friendly and encouraging. That is the most common way this process fails. Friendly is not evidence — a commitment is.

## 18. Spain and Andalucía — the regulatory reality

Added in v1.1 to test the risks raised in vision §17. These are **questions to ask operators**, not statements of law. Where a regime is named, treat it as a prompt to verify with a Spanish *gestor* or travel-law specialist — rules and commencement dates move.

Run this block with S1, S3, S5 and S6. Skip it with freelance guides unless they invoice independently.

### 18.1 Licensing and registration

- "Are you registered in the *Registro de Turismo de Andalucía*? Under which category?"
- "Did you register by *declaración responsable*, or did someone do it for you?"
- "Who in your operation holds the official Andalucía guide *habilitación*? Can non-licensed people guide any part of what you sell?"
- "Which sites can you not enter without a licensed guide?"

*Why it matters:* determines whether an operator is already inside a regulated frame, which changes both their risk appetite and what we may safely automate on their behalf.

### 18.2 Intermediation and combined services — the package question

This tests vision §17.2 and gates the network routing fee.

- "When you sell a tour **plus** a transfer, or a tour plus a hotel night — who is the contracting party on the invoice?"
- "Has anyone ever told you that combining those makes you a travel agency?"
- "Do you hold *responsabilidad civil* insurance? Has anyone asked you for insolvency protection or a bond?"
- "When you pass a client to a Granada operator, who is legally responsible if it goes wrong? Has that ever been tested?"
- "If a client cancels a combined day, who refunds what?"

*Listen for:* whether operators already avoid combining services **on purpose**. If they do, that is a strong signal that the routing-fee model in vision §11 sits in regulated territory and needs advice before design, not after.

### 18.3 Invoicing and certified software

Spain has been phasing in requirements for computerised invoicing systems under the antifraud law and its implementing regulation, commonly discussed as *Verifactu*. **Confirm current scope and dates with a gestor — do not rely on this document.**

- "What software issues your *facturas*?"
- "Has your gestor mentioned Verifactu or the antifraud invoicing rules? What did they tell you to do?"
- "How often does a company ask for a *factura completa* with CIF rather than a *simplificada*?"
- "When you refund, who issues the *factura rectificativa*?"

*Why it matters:* vision §3 row 14 proposes generating invoices from the booking record. If certified-software obligations apply, that is a licensed-vendor problem, not a feature we can casually ship. **This question can kill a roadmap item cheaply.**

### 18.4 WhatsApp — whose number is it

This tests vision §17.1, the platform-dependency risk.

- "Is that a personal number or a WhatsApp Business account?"
- "Who else has access to it? What happens when you are on a tour?"
- "Has WhatsApp ever blocked or limited you?"
- "If you sold the business tomorrow, what happens to that number and those conversations?"
- "Have you ever tried to send a template or bulk message? What happened?"
- "If a tool needed you to use a *different* number, would you?" — last resort; it is a hypothetical, so weight it lightly.

*Listen for:* the answer to the last-but-one question is the real one. An operator whose entire history lives in a personal WhatsApp thread has a continuity problem they have not noticed — and it is the same problem we would be creating for ourselves.

### 18.5 Personal data, ID custody and image rights

- "Where do the passport numbers you collect for the Alhambra end up? Who keeps them, and for how long?"
- "Has a guest ever asked you to delete their data? What did you actually do?"
- "Do you provide transport or vehicles as well as guiding?" — if yes, ask whether any traveller-registration reporting obligation applies to them; verify separately.
- "Have you ever asked a guest for written permission to use their photo?"
- "What do you do when there are children in the photo?"
- "Has a client ever asked you *not* to photograph someone in their group?"

*Why it matters:* Spanish image rights sit under their own regime (*derecho a la propia imagen*) alongside GDPR, and the supervisory authority is the AEPD. The last question is the sharpest one in this entire guide for a specific reason — see §19.6.

### 18.6 Reviews and platform policy

This tests the correction made in vision §5.3.

- "Show me how you ask for a review."
- "Have you ever offered anything — a discount, a drink, a photo — in exchange for a review?"
- "Has Google or Tripadvisor ever removed your reviews, or warned you?"
- "How much of your business do you think comes from your Google listing?"

*Listen for:* if operators have been burned by review removal, they will be **more** receptive to a design that keeps a gift and a review request structurally separate — and actively hostile to anything that looks like an incentive.

### 18.7 Cost base — the question that decides the business model

- "What do you pay each month between *cuota de autónomos* and *gestoría*?"
- "What software do you pay for monthly, and what does it cost in total?"
- "What was the last thing you cancelled because it was not worth the money?"

*Why it matters:* this is the single best test of vision §11's "not SaaS-first" decision. An operator carrying a high fixed monthly base before earning a euro will reject subscriptions and may accept success fees. **Get the actual number.**

## 19. The Andalusian operating calendar and geography

These conditions do not exist in a generic European market and they shape the product more than any feature decision.

### 19.1 Monument constraints

- "How far ahead do you book Alhambra tickets? What happens when they are gone?"
- "Who actually buys them — you, the client, or a partner?"
- "Tell me about the last time someone arrived without the ID that was on the ticket."
- "Alcázar, Mezquita, Alhambra — which one causes you the most trouble and why?"

### 19.2 Semana Santa and Feria

- "What happens to your tours during Semana Santa?"
- "Do you sell more, or do you stop?"
- "How do you handle street closures and monument closures that change day to day?"

### 19.3 August

- "What did you do in the August heat this year?"
- "Did you cancel or reschedule anything for heat? Who decided?"
- "What do you tell guests to bring?"

*Why it matters:* vision §3 row 13 assumes August is dead revenue to be filled with vouchers and memory books. Find out whether operators experience August as a problem or as a rest they want.

### 19.4 Cruise calls

- "Do you take shore-excursion work from Cádiz or Málaga?"
- "What happens when the ship is late, or does not call at all?"
- "Who do you contract with — the line, an agent, or the passenger?"

### 19.5 The multi-city ring

- "Granada, Córdoba, Ronda, Jerez — which do you do yourself and which do you pass on?"
- "Who drives? Is the vehicle yours?"
- "What is the longest day you will accept?"

### 19.6 Gulf and Arabic-language clients

The highest-value segment, and the one that most directly tests the Guide's Gift.

- "Do you work with clients from the Gulf? How do they find you?"
- "What changes operationally — timings, food, prayer, Ramadan?"
- "Who guides in Arabic? Is that hard to staff?"
- **"Have clients ever asked you not to photograph the women in the group?"**

*Why this is the sharpest question in the guide:* vision §5.3 proposes that a guide photographs guests and sends it to them. If a high-value, high-margin segment routinely declines to be photographed, the Guide's Gift needs a **non-photo path** — voice and text only — or it excludes exactly the clients who would pay most for the resulting keepsake. Nothing in the vision document anticipated this.

### 19.7 Licensed-guide supply

- "When did you last fail to find a licensed guide? What did you do?"
- "Which language do you turn work away in?"
- "How do you find guides — associations, WhatsApp groups, personal network?"
- "What do guides complain to you about?"

*Why it matters:* vision §12 claims guides are a second distribution channel. Find out where guides actually congregate in Seville before assuming we can reach them.
