## 1. The decision

**Our primary objective is validation and design-partner discovery — not closing sales on the floor.**

200 exhibitors are selling. 8,000 people are being sold to. A pre-revenue product with a development-only demo competing for floor attention is expensive noise.

But the [interview guide](./operator-interview-guide-2026-09.md) §17 says we need operator interviews and guide conversations before the thesis earns the right to control anything. In Seville that normally takes a season of coffees. At FIBES it takes three days — in our home city, in our languages, at no travel cost.

**We remain open to qualified follow-up opportunities. We will not distort an interview into a sales pitch.** If a serious operator or platform wants a pilot conversation, take it — and book it for after the event, when we can scope it properly.

**The people we need are not on the confirmed-names list.** That list is enterprise. Our buyers are the solo operators, mid-size firms and licensed guides walking the floor as visitors.

### Success metrics — note there is no sales target

| Target | Primary | Stretch |
|---|---|---|
| Substantive operator interviews | 8–12 | 15 |
| Licensed-guide conversations (Gate 3 — the load-bearing bet) | 3 | 5 |
| Credible design-partner candidates | 1–2 | 3 |
| Structured follow-ups booked for after the event | 2–3 | 5 |
| Expert leads on Verifactu and package exposure | 2 | — |
| **Claims made that we cannot support** | **0** | **0** |

**Fifteen interviews is roughly 10 hours of interview time before demos, walking a 200-exhibitor hall, note capture and sessions.** Treat 15 as a stretch. The failure mode is counting shallow five-minute chats to hit a number, which destroys the data.

**A completed interview contains all eight of these.** Anything less is a conversation, and should be logged as one:

1. A recent, real enquiry example
2. Their current workflow
3. The repeated clarification burden, quantified
4. Current tools
5. What went wrong or consumed time
6. Reaction to the playback
7. One falsification question
8. A concrete next step, or explicit disinterest

**"Expert lead" means** a Spanish tax adviser or qualified invoicing specialist, or a lawyer familiar with Spanish and EU package-travel rules — **not an operator's opinion.** TIS can identify the question and the expert. It cannot deliver a legal conclusion.

### Why no booth

A booth costs money we do not have and — worse — puts us in selling mode, which is the wrong mode for this trip. Visitor passes, a laptop, and pre-booked meetings.

## 2. Product vision

> **Para Usted turns a traveller's conversation into a traveller-reviewed Journey Brief and a deliberate Trip Plan, and prepares that context for the professional who decides what is possible.**

Floor version, three clauses, repeatable by a stranger:

> ### We organise. The traveller decides. The operator confirms.
> *Nosotros organizamos. El viajero decide. El operador confirma.*

**"Prepares", not "hands" or "sends".** The playback does not transmit anything to a real operator, and our own core message is *"Nothing has been sent."* When real recipient selection and explicit sending exist, the verb can change. Not before.

**Why this and not something bigger.** Many travel AI products are optimised to *generate* — itineraries, prices, packages. The industry's characteristic failure is confident fabrication, and an operator's deepest fear is being bound to a promise a machine made on their behalf. We invert the emphasis: organise what the traveller actually said, mark what is still open, and stop where human judgement begins.

**We begin with an artefact: the Journey Brief.** Travel has the invoice, the boarding pass, the voucher. It has no standard way for a traveller's *intent* to reach an operator undistorted. We are testing whether the Journey Brief can become that standard handoff — we do not claim to own a category that does not yet exist.

### The ladder — we present rungs 1 and 2 only

| Rung | Status | At TIS |
|---|---|---|
| **1.** Journey Brief + Trip Plan; suggested ≠ saved ≠ requested ≠ confirmed; never invents price or availability | **Built. In the demo.** | Yes — this is the pitch |
| **2.** The brief reaches the operator and reduces real clarification work; the guide contributes something back | Hypothesis (Gates 2–3) | Yes — labelled as what we came to test |
| **3.** The thread persists into a traveller-controlled record — the Memory Layer | Unvalidated (Gates 4–6) | **Only if asked**, flagged as a bet |

If asked where it goes: *"If operators tell us the brief is worth something, the same thread can keep going after the tour. We haven't earned the right to claim that yet — that's part of why we're here."*

### The refusals are the product

Provable, and phrased as what the product does today:

- We do not invent a price or its commercial meaning. We show the operator's published price.
- We do not claim availability.
- We do not invent a package or an itinerary.
- We do not treat a suggestion as a confirmed itinerary.
- We do not save or send without the traveller's explicit action.
- We do not present a saved possibility as booked, or a prepared request as sent.
- We do not speak for the operator.
- Booking and inventory remain with the operator's existing system.

**Two of these are constitutional and stay absolute:** we do not resell, and we take no inventory. That is what makes us not an OTA, and softening it would discard the strongest boundary we have.

### The headline

Provable claim:

> **"We show the operator's published price. We never invent one."**
> *"Mostramos el precio publicado por el operador. Nunca inventamos uno."*

Memorable hook for the floor:

> **"Our AI knows where to stop."**
> *"Nuestra IA sabe cuándo detenerse."*

**Do not use "every other AI here…" in any form.** We have not inspected hundreds of products, and it is the same absolutism we retracted in vision v1.1 §17.4. Differentiate without attacking exhibitors we know nothing about.

**Price basis.** Áloratur's private Caminito is **€180 per group**, not per person. The screen says which. Never quote it without the basis.

| "We have N operators onboard" | Two granted permission to **display published data**. Neither has signed anything. Say "two operators agreed to be shown" |
| "We're partnered with Áloratur" | Permission to display ≠ partnership. Say "shown with their permission" |

### The seven questions, with answers

1. **"How is this different from Mindtrip or ChatGPT?"** → "Many planning products focus on generating recommendations and itineraries. We focus on the handoff between traveller context and operator judgement — making suggested, saved, prepared, sent and confirmed explicitly different states."
2. **"So you're an OTA?"** → No. We never take inventory, never resell, never hold the booking.
3. **"What's your traction?"** → "A working, operator-grounded demonstration and no validated market traction. We're here to test whether the problem and the mechanism are valuable." Say it plainly — it reads as confidence.
4. **"Who pays?"** → Hypothesis: operator success fee on direct bookings we originate, never on your OTA volume. Not fixed until tested.
5. **"Isn't this just a lead form?"** → A lead form asks *our* questions. A brief captures *theirs*, in their words, and states what is still unknown.
6. **"What about GDPR?"** → "This playback neither stores nor sends any traveller information. A real pilot would need purpose-bounded records, explicit recipient choice, data minimisation, retention rules and legal review — none of which we're claiming to have built."
7. **"Can it book?"** → "It can take the traveller into the operator's existing booking flow. Availability and booking stay in the operator's system — we don't hold either."

## 4. Demo runbook

### Setup

```
cd C:\learn\seville-tours
pnpm exec next dev --port 3100
```
Open `http://localhost:3100/en/tis-showcase/playback`.

**Always `/en/`.** The showcase components carry no i18n — `locale` is forwarded only to the recommendation receipt. Loading `/es/` renders an English UI and looks broken. **Narrate in Spanish; leave the screen in English**, and say so if asked: *"the playback is English; the wider product runs in four languages."*

**The route is development-only by design** (`notFound()` outside development). Run it locally. Do not weaken the route guard to make deployment easier.

### Development-mode protocol — mandatory before each meeting block

Next dev mode brings compilation pauses, the dev-tools indicator and hot reload. None of that should appear in front of an operator.

- [ ] Start the server **before entering FIBES**, not in front of someone
- [ ] **Pre-warm the route** at the start of each block so no compilation pause is visible
- [ ] Clean browser profile — no extensions, no bookmarks bar, no personal tabs
- [ ] Notifications off at the OS level
- [ ] Terminal minimised
- [ ] **Never update dependencies at the event**

### Offline resilience — verified by code, to be verified by rehearsal

`getMarcoTours()` reads commerce facts from the pre-generated manifest at `public/generated/wordpress-tour-manifest.json` and **never fetches at runtime**; `extractItemId` is a pure string parse; the playback route renders no remote images.

**That is an inference from code, not a result.** Before we rely on it, run the full protocol: restart the laptop → disable networking → start the server → clean browser profile → hard-refresh the route → confirm fonts and styling load → confirm €50 and the Trip Plan render → complete the whole micro-demo. Then, and only then, record internally: *"the playback is verified to run without internet access."*

**Do not announce on the floor that other exhibitors' demos are down.** Just enjoy the resilience.

### Version A — 30-second hook, for passers-by

| # | Action | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Start** | "A traveller has a few hours in Seville." |
| 2 | **Next** | "This is the Journey Brief — organised from what she actually said. That's the operator's published price. We didn't author it." |
| 3 | **Add to Trip Plan** | "She decides. Not the AI." |
| 4 | *(point at panel)* | "Saved — and it says **nothing booked**." |

Purpose: earn interest and invite the longer version. Stop. Ask a question.

### Version B — 55–70 seconds, the operator version

**Version A proves traveller control. It does not prove the operator proposition — which is the business.** Anyone who might be a customer gets Version B.

Continue from step 4: `Next` → `Next`

| Beat | What you say |
|---|---|
| Private refinement appears as a **separate** request | "She's changed what she wants. That's a different thing, so it's a different item — we don't merge them." |
| "Nothing has been sent" | "Still nothing sent. No operator has accepted anything." |
| Operator review | **"This is where the vague enquiry becomes something the operator can review without reconstructing the entire conversation."** |

That last line is the most important sentence on the floor. Land it.

### Version C — full playback, seated meetings only, 90–120s

`Start` → `Next` → `Add to Trip Plan` → `Next` → `Next` → pick a staged operator action → `Next`

Beats to land: refinement creates a **separate** request ("we don't merge things that aren't the same thing") → "Nothing sent. No operator has accepted." → the operator review shows only the private request, with the Alcázar marked as a separate saved possibility.

### NEVER CLICK

> **"Check dates before booking"** and **"Check dates"** are real FareHarbor links with no `target="_blank"`.
> Playback state is in-memory only. **Clicking either navigates away and destroys the entire demo.**
> If you need to reference them, point — don't click.

### Recovery

- Demo misbehaves → **Reset**.
- Navigated away → browser Back won't restore state. Reload the URL and re-run; a clean start takes ~10 seconds.
- Primary laptop dies → backup device with the recorded demo. Both dead → screenshots on the phone.

### Each beat maps to a claim

| What they see | What it proves |
|---|---|
| Journey Brief assembles | We organise what was said |
| €50, unit-neutral, operator-published | We don't invent price |
| Traveller taps Add | The traveller decides, not the AI |
| "Nothing booked" | We don't fake commitment |
| Refinement → separate request | We don't merge unlike things |
| "Nothing sent. No operator has accepted." | We don't speak for the operator |
| Operator review | The human confirms |

## 6. Floor tactics

**Fifteen interviews over three days is five a day at 35–45 minutes plus walking.** Achievable for two people. Impossible for one. Confirm the second person this week.

### Who we're hunting, in priority order

1. **Licensed freelance guides** — Gate 3, the load-bearing bet. Cannot be validated by talking to owners. Three minimum.
2. **Solo bespoke operators** — the stated beachhead. Six.
3. **Mid-size firms** — two.
4. **OTA-dependent operators** — two. The hardest conversation and therefore the most valuable.
5. **One luxury/DMC, one network broker.**

### The confirmed enterprise names — how to think about them

- **Klook** — experiences OTA. We explicitly don't compete; we don't resell. A market-education conversation, not a sale.
- **Skyscanner** — metasearch, relevant to agentic booking. Listen, don't pitch.
- **Grupo Piñero / Louvre Hotels** — they have guests with unstructured in-destination intent and the same "we lose the guest" problem. Interesting, **but a distraction from the beachhead.** Note it. Don't chase it.
- **WeRoad** — not our segment.

### Discipline

- Open with Version A. If they could be a customer, continue into Version B. Then **stop pitching and start interviewing.**
- **Never open an interview with "how many messages does it take you?"** It presupposes our preferred problem. That line is a *floor hook*, not a research question.
- **The opening question is always:** *"Can you walk me through the most recent bespoke enquiry you handled, from the first message until you could respond properly?"* Then follow with: what information was missing · how many exchanges · which part took longest · what the traveller changed · did it convert · what tool held the final context · what would have helped · **what should software never decide for you.**
- Always close with a commitment ask — time, an introduction, or a scoped follow-up. A "yes, but later" is a no.
- **Do not ask anyone for access to live traveller enquiries at the event.** The staged path is: they confirm the pain → they agree to a follow-up → we scope a bounded test → data-sharing and privacy boundaries are agreed → only then are real enquiries discussed. We are the consent-discipline company; behave like it.
- Collect contact details with a stated purpose and a way to be forgotten.

## 9. After TIS

Within one week, before the memory fades:

1. **Score the interviews against the traceability matrix** ([interview guide](./operator-interview-guide-2026-09.md) §16). Which assumptions survived, which died.
2. **Judge the wedge on effort, not message count alone.** Five long, difficult exchanges can be more painful than ten quick templates. Evaluate together: number of exchanges · elapsed time to a proper response · missing-information frequency · lost enquiries · misunderstood requests · commercial value of bespoke enquiries · how well their existing workaround performs · willingness to test · willingness to pay.

   **The wedge fails if** the pain is uncommon, an existing form already solves it, operators do not value the brief, operators will not accept a structured handoff, travellers will not complete or review it, or the improvement does not justify the integration effort or the fee. If any two of those hold across the interviews, say so out loud.
3. **If Gate 3 fails** — guides will not contribute post-tour — amend the vision document the same way we amended it for v1.1: in public, in the corrections register, not quietly.
4. **Convert design-partner commitments into one dated pilot** with before/after numbers on messages-to-booking, direct share, no-show rate, review rate and repeat rate. Vision §12: one credible case study sells the next twenty.

**The failure mode to guard against:** coming home with 40 business cards, a good feeling, and nothing falsified. Friendly is not evidence. A commitment is.

## 10. Before TIS — the Seville Tours Co. meeting

**Hold this in the Sep 19–22 window. It is more valuable than most of the fifteen meetings at FIBES**, because Seville Tours Co. is the operator whose actual catalogue is inside our demo — the Alcázar tour, the €50, the FareHarbor item, the manifest. He can answer things nobody else can. And if he says yes to his guides, Gate 3 is de-risked before we even arrive.

### The danger: a friend will be encouraging, and encouragement is worthless data

Open with these two sentences. They are the most useful thing in the meeting.

> *"I need you to try to talk me out of this. If you're nice to me I'll waste six months. Tell me why an operator would say no."*

> *"How much have you already heard about what I'm building?"*

The first converts criticism from an insult into a favour. The second establishes contamination — if he has already seen the demo, his answers on the wedge are weak evidence and the clean signal must come from strangers at FIBES.

### A. Product truth only he can confirm

Our demo shows **his** data. If any of it is stale, we present wrong numbers at TIS. Go in with the hypothesis and ask him to confirm or correct, rather than asking blind.

| What we believe | Confirm / correct |
|---|---|
| Alcázar tour, adult base €50 | |
| Child €10 · Infant €5 · Student €35 · Senior €35 | |
| FareHarbor adds a booking fee on top of base | |
| ID / passport mandatory at the monument | |
| The FareHarbor link in our demo is still the live flow | |

- [ ] **"If someone at TIS asks me 'is that €50 per person' — what is the correct answer?"**

That last question matters more than it looks. Our demo deliberately shows €50 unit-neutral. If a sharp operator presses us on the floor and we guess, we have broken our own central claim about not inventing.

### B. Baseline numbers — capture now or lose them forever

If he becomes the pilot, these are the "before" column in the only case study that will sell the next twenty operators. **Once we ship anything, they are gone.** Write them verbatim; do not round.

- [ ] "Open your phone — the last private booking you closed. How many messages?"
- [ ] "How many enquiries last month? How many converted?"
- [ ] "What did you pay in OTA commission last season?"
- [ ] "How many no-shows or late cancellations?"
- [ ] "How many reviews last season, and how many came from you asking?"
- [ ] "Has any guest ever come back, or booked you in another city?"
- [ ] "How many gift vouchers did you sell last year?"

### C. His guides — the highest-value ask in the meeting

**Gate 3 is the load-bearing bet of the entire vision and cannot be validated by talking to owners.** He has guides. Nobody else we know does.

- [ ] "Who guides for you? Employed or freelance? How many?"
- [ ] **"Can I buy three of them a coffee before TIS?"**
- [ ] "What's the last thing you asked a guide to do that they quietly stopped doing?"
- [ ] "Do your guides take photos on tour? Do guests ever ask them for one?"
- [ ] "Has a guest ever asked a guide to write something down — a name, a date, a story?"

### D. TIS-specific and time-sensitive

- [ ] **"Are you going to TIS?"** If yes, he is our floor guide and our credibility.
- [ ] "Which Seville operators should I talk to? Would you introduce me?"
- [ ] **"Can I say we work with you? Can I show your tour and use your name?"** — an explicit yes or no. **This is a claims-sheet item**; we cannot imply a partnership he has not agreed to.
- [ ] Read the §3 claims sheet aloud: *"Which of these would make a real operator roll their eyes?"*
- [ ] "What will operators at TIS be cynical about that I'm not expecting?"

### E. The regulatory answers we currently lack

Two of our §1 success metrics live here. He may have both.

- [ ] "Are you registered in the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía? Under what category?"
- [ ] "What software issues your facturas? Has your gestor mentioned **Verifactu**?"
- [ ] "When you sell a tour plus a transfer, who is the contracting party? Has anyone told you that makes you a travel agency?"
- [ ] "Where do the passport numbers you collect actually end up, and who keeps them?"
- [ ] "What do you pay monthly between cuota de autónomos and gestoría?"

That last number decides whether our business model is a subscription or a success fee. It is worth the meeting on its own.

### Then — the dress rehearsal

Run the 45-second micro-demo on him. **If it does not land with the operator whose own catalogue is in it, it will not land with a stranger.**

Watch, do not narrate. Afterwards ask only three things:

- "What did you think it was claiming?"
- "What did you expect to happen when I tapped Add?"
- "Where did you stop paying attention?"

**Watch where his eyes go when you tap Add.** If they do not move to the Trip Plan panel, that is F1 confirmed in the field and the strongest possible argument for fixing it before Oct 6.

### Close with an escalating ask

1. **Time** — "Can I sit with you one morning and watch enquiries come in?"
2. **Reputation** — "Will you introduce me to three operators and three guides?"
3. **Access** — "Will you let me handle your next five enquiries and measure it against your normal?"

Take the highest one he says yes to. *"Yes, definitely, let's talk after TIS"* is a no — log it as one.

### Logging

Count him as one of the fifteen, but **mark the row "warm source"**. His interview is deep but biased. Spend it extracting what only he can give: the price truth, the baseline numbers, the guides, and the introductions.
